About Me

I am a Data Scientist with a PhD in Computer Science and experience in machine learning, deep learning, data analysis and explainable AI. My work combines the development of predictive models with the design of tools to explore, visualize and understand complex data.

I have worked on applied machine learning projects addressing different challenges, from predicting leaks in drinking water networks to identifying and analyzing urban pico-climatic patterns from mobile sensor data.

Throughout my academic and professional experience, I have worked with different types of data: image and sound data during my Master's degree, image and tabular data during my PhD, and spatio-temporal data during my postdoctoral research. This has given me experience adapting data analysis and machine learning approaches to different data modalities and application contexts.

I am particularly interested in turning complex data into actionable insights, while paying attention to model reliability, interpretability and computational efficiency.

I completed a postdoctoral project at the LIRMM (University of Montpellier) in 2024–2026. I obtained my PhD at the LaBRI (University of Bordeaux) in 2024, with a thesis on visualization for the explanation of deep neural networks. I previously graduated from the University of Bordeaux's Master's degree in Computer Science (Image and Sound) in 2020 and received the Isabelle Attali Prize in 2018.

Spatio-temporal Data Analysis & Urban Microclimates

Development of a web application to analyze spatio-temporal data collected by mobile sensors and identify and characterize urban pico-climates.

An interactive clustering algorithm is integrated to group meteorological measurements into clusters representing typical urban configurations, referred to as pico-climates. SHAP explanations are further incorporated to identify and interpret the key factors influencing the assignment of measurements to these clusters.

Focus: Spatio-temporal data · Unsupervised learning · Data visualization · Web application

Machine Learning for Water Leak Prediction

During my PhD, in collaboration with the SUEZ innovation center Le LyRE, I worked on predicting leaks in the drinking water distribution network of Bordeaux using convolutional neural networks (CNNs).

I also worked on reducing model complexity: more than 95% of the parameters and 93% of the computation could be pruned without loss of performance, resulting in an execution time divided by five.

Focus: Predictive modeling · Deep learning · Model optimization · Explainability

Explainable AI & Deep Learning Visualization

My PhD research focused on visualization techniques to help understand and explain deep neural network predictions, compare classification models and inspect network pruning.

This work led to several research contributions, including:

  • Relative Confusion Matrix (RCM) to visually compare two classification models at the class level.
  • H²O (Heatmap by Hierarchical Occlusion) to visualize the impact of image pixels on model predictions.
  • NetPrune to analyze neural network components and their contribution to predictions.

Publications and code are available in the section below.

Focus: Explainable AI · Information visualization · Deep learning · Model comparison
